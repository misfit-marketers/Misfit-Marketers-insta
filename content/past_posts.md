## MARKETINGTIP#1: Which is the most addictive colour and why?

1. (hook) Do you know which is the most addictive colour and why?
2. Humans' association with the colour **red** can be dated back to the days when they lived in caves and hunted for food.
3. In such primal times, things that caught a caveman's attention were **fire** (heat), **blushing** (attraction), **rage**, and **blood** (danger); all of which were red.
4. These things led to humans being wired in such a way that the colour red **increases our heart rate** and **catches our attention** first.
5. Soon, brands realised the importance of the colour red and leveraged it. For example, **curvy sports cars** and **alluring outfits** are made in red for the very same reason.
6. Another brand that continues to use the colour red is Facebook. They pioneered the **red notification icon**.
7. Takeaway: Use the colour red at the right place to draw your prospect's attention to something important such as a **CTA** or your service's **USP**.

## MARKETINGTIP#2: Is your marketing actually driving customers away?

1. (hook) Is your marketing actually driving customers away?
2. There is a phenomenon in psychology known as **aversiveness**.
3. **What is aversiveness?** In general, aversiveness refers to avoidant behaviour towards something. For example, people who are not risk takers are averse to investing in stocks.
4. **What is aversiveness in marketing?** It is when a person sees a marketing activity, such as an advertisement or an email, and immediately feels the need to avoid it or get rid of it.
5. **Why does it happen?** Aversive behaviour happens when marketing feels forced, like an unsolicited sales pitch. It's the same reason people feel annoyed when a bank calls asking if you'd like a credit card.
6. **We conducted an experiment** and sent a newsletter to nearly 2,000 recipients with two ways to reach our website: a 'Visit Website' button, or a plain link in the text.
7. **Results of the experiment?** The plain link did 3x better than the button. Button: 70 clicks. Link: 210 clicks.
8. **Experiment interpretation:** Prospects feel that clicking a CTA button is a big commitment, while a plain link feels more transparent and involves less commitment.
9. **Takeaway:** Avoid elements that make it seem like you're shoving your product down the prospect's throat. Give prospects resources to educate themselves. Be transparent, and don't ask for commitment too quickly.

## MARKETINGTIP#4: Are you losing revenue by offering free services?

1. (hook) Are you losing revenue by offering free services?
2. It used to be ordinary practice for brands to offer their services **free during the first month**, or to price their cheapest plan at **$0** to get more customers.
3. But now, brands are slowly moving away from the freemium model because **they discovered a flaw** in it.
4. When customers see a price of $0, **they assume its value is equivalent to 0**. And when they are asked to pay (eg: an upsell), they are reluctant since they are used to paying nothing.
5. In psychology, this is linked to the **primacy effect**: the tendency to remember the first piece of information about a subject. In simple words, the **first impression**.
6. So once customers have made up their mind that it's free, **it will be hard to convince them to pay**.
7. To counter this, brands have started clearly calling it a **'14-day trial'** instead of 'first month free'. And this worked better!
8. **Takeaway:** Go for free pricing if you're looking to increase your rating or need feedback on your product. If your goal is generating revenue, avoid the freemium pricing model.

## MARKETINGTIP#5: How long should you run your ads?

1. (hook) How long should you run your ads?
2. **Did you know?** Babies smile more at people who smile at them.
3. Researchers found that babies feel comfortable around people they are **more familiar with**.
4. This came to be known as the **mere-exposure effect**.
5. It is a psychological phenomenon where **people prefer things they have seen often**, because they feel familiar.
6. The same applies to advertising. The more people see the same ad, the more they begin to subconsciously have an **affinity** with it.
7. Brands like Apple or Sony did not get to where they are with one or two ads. They look at advertising as an investment.
8. **Takeaway:** Give your ads time to work. Familiarity builds over repeated exposure, so judge an ad on several weeks of data.

## MARKETINGTIP#6: Eenie, Meenie, Minie, Mo

1. (hook) Eenie, Meenie, Minie, Mo
2. Humans are interesting creatures. We say we want multiple options to choose from, but **do we really?**
3. Shown a cluttered TV remote next to a minimal one, or a busy website next to a clean one, most of us prefer the less cluttered, minimal option. This is explained by the **'paradox of choice'**.
4. The 'paradox of choice' says that with more options, we take longer to decide and end up feeling **overwhelmed, stressed, and anxious**.
5. To avoid this, businesses reduce the number of ways a customer could get distracted. This **reduces friction in the customer journey**.
6. **Takeaway:** Use a maximum of 2 CTAs. Use subpages instead of crowding the homepage. Offer up to 3 pricing plans, not more.

## MARKETERSASSEMBLE#1: Marketers vs The world

1. (hook) Marketers vs The world
2. To be a marketer is to acknowledge that the outcome is **uncertain**.
3. In a world run by volatile customer patterns and AI-powered social media, you can never say with 100% confidence how a prospect will perceive your content or how the algorithm works.
4. Unless you **experiment**.
5. Experiment with the copywriting of the ad, the targeting of paid campaigns, the content of a landing page, the SEO of an article.
6. This is something non-marketers often miss: freelancers, entrepreneurs, designers, developers, or anyone who hasn't learned the **theory behind marketing**.
7. As marketers, we understand the uncertainty of the prospect's rationale and the algorithm, and get better over time by **trying again and again**.
8. We don't assume what prospects like, we **find it out**.
